# Journal and article delivery

Direct `/blog` and `/blog/:id` requests now return readable public content, canonical metadata and Blog/BlogPosting structured data. Stable article IDs survive title changes. Existing hash links continue to resolve. Unknown or unpublished article IDs return 404; unavailable sources and malformed successful responses return 503 with noindex and Retry-After. Successful HTML is no-store and GET/HEAD only.

The public archive subscription transfers summaries rather than every article body. The article view resolves its exact published ID independently and honors withdrawal, transport failure and retry. The existing 250-post archive bound remains; larger-archive pagination is separate future work. Admin queries remain independently authorized and unchanged.

The backend detail query returns a sanitized contentHtml field, and the initial-document renderer defensively rebuilds markup through the same parsed allowlist. The client renders that public field without downloading the parser. Text, headings, lists, quotes, tables, public images and safe links survive. Scripts, event handlers, arbitrary style, forms, embedded active content and foreign namespaces do not. Internal fragment IDs receive a journal prefix to avoid application DOM collisions. A one-million-character article limit and a 100-level rendering depth limit prevent unbounded rendering; unsupported content is never silently treated as a healthy article response. The existing parse5 version is now an explicit production dependency. Neither the archive nor article client module imports the parser.

Initial HTML remains visible through module/data loading. Live success, failure or withdrawal removes it; navigation and the error boundary remove stale documents and schema. Article metadata belongs to the article query rather than the generic application effect. Missing client-side articles receive temporary noindex, removed on recovery or leaving the page. Native story anchors support keyboard activation, modifier clicks and history. Article navigation focuses its heading. Index-card images are decorative within the already-labeled story link. Existing newsletter and sharing actions remain available.

Validation covers anonymous query boundaries, draft omission and withdrawal, malformed and unavailable responses, escaping and sanitization, direct/hash navigation and metadata cleanup. The browser fixture exercises archive/article accessibility at 320, 844 and 1440 pixels, keyboard activation, focus, Back, withdrawal and recovery with zero business commands. Separately, the currently published production article was read without mutation and rendered locally with JavaScript disabled at 390 and 1440 pixels: both archive and article have one heading, no overflow or accessibility violations. A redundant image-alt finding was fixed and its earlier evidence retained. These are candidate checks, not a production release or repeated Lighthouse acceptance.

CI includes the synthetic browser fixture and unbundled Node runtime checks. The route registry includes the journal and the verified published article identity. Deploy the backend functions and frontend, confirm both successful releases, then verify direct HTTP responses, live article readiness and repeated controlled measurements before declaring this phase accepted.
